Common questions

FAQ

What is TDEE and why does it matter? ▾

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure — the total number of calories your body burns in a day. It accounts for your basal metabolic rate (the calories you burn at rest) plus the energy used during physical activity. Knowing your TDEE is the foundation of any nutrition plan: eat at TDEE to maintain weight, below it to lose fat, or above it to gain muscle. Without this number, you're guessing.

How are my macros calculated? ▾

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation — the most accurate BMR formula validated in clinical research. Your BMR is multiplied by an activity factor to give your TDEE, then adjusted up or down based on your goal. Macros are then set as follows:

Protein: 2.0 g per kg of bodyweight — the evidence-based range for supporting muscle retention and growth.
Fat: 0.8 g per kg — the minimum required for healthy hormone production and fat-soluble vitamin absorption.
Carbs: All remaining calories (after protein and fat are accounted for) divided by 4 calories per gram.

How much protein do I actually need? ▾

For most people pursuing fat loss or muscle gain, the research consistently points to 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day. This calculator uses 2.0 g/kg — the upper-middle of that range — because it provides a comfortable buffer to protect muscle during a calorie deficit, and to maximise muscle protein synthesis when eating at maintenance or above.

The old recommendation of 0.8 g/kg is for sedentary individuals with no body composition goals. If you're training, you need significantly more.

Why might these numbers differ from other calculators? ▾

Different calculators use different BMR formulas. The two most common are Harris-Benedict (1919, revised 1984) and Mifflin-St Jeor (1990). Multiple studies comparing the two have found Mifflin-St Jeor to be more accurate for the general population — which is why this calculator uses it.

Macro allocations also vary. Some calculators use fixed percentage splits (e.g. 30/40/30). We set protein first based on bodyweight, fat second as a minimum floor, then fill carbs with the remainder — which is more aligned with how sports dietitians actually prescribe macros for body composition goals.
