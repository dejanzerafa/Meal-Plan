# SoulGainz Brand & Pricing Analysis
**Date:** August 2026 | **Framework:** Jung Brand Archetypes + Competitive Benchmarking

---

## 1. Brand Identity

### Archetype
**Primary: Hero (60–70%)**
SoulGainz is built around mastery, achievement, and transformation through discipline. The language is direct and performance-oriented: "Fuel your gainz", "training hard", "one cook session, seven days sorted." The Hero brand promises results, not comfort.

**Secondary: Sage (30–40%)**
The Sage archetype shows through the emphasis on verified science and precision: "Macros verified to the gram", "evidence-based", curated recipe library with specific protein targets. Users trust SoulGainz because it knows things — it doesn't guess, it calculates.

This is a powerful combination. Hero drives aspiration and desire; Sage provides credibility and justification for the premium price.

### Brand Voice
- **Tone:** Direct, confident, no filler. "It was chaos." — Two words, full stop.
- **Style:** Short sentences. Specific claims. Numbers matter. ("170+", "to the gram", "85+")
- **Personality:** Disciplined, ambitious, no-nonsense. Speaks to people who train seriously.
- **NOT:** Peppy wellness influencer copy. NOT "nourish your body". NOT "every journey starts with one step."

### Visual Identity
| Element | Value | Assessment |
|---------|-------|------------|
| Background | `#0C0B0A` near-black | Strong, premium, gym-aesthetic |
| Accent | `#E07B2A` warm orange | Energy, action, appetite — well chosen |
| Body text | `#F2EDE6` warm cream | Warmer than pure white — approachable |
| Muted text | `#8A8580` grey | Good hierarchy |
| Typography | Inter 900 weight | Heavy, confident — matches archetype |

**Assessment:** Visual identity is consistent and well-executed. The dark/orange palette differentiates from the green/blue wellness space. It reads as a *performance* app, not a *diet* app. This is correct positioning for the target audience.

## 2. Target Audience

Based on brand signals and recipe library content:

- **Age:** 22–38 (primary), 38–45 (secondary)
- **Goal:** Muscle gain, body recomposition, or maintained physique through busy schedule
- **Lifestyle:** Trains 4–6x/week. Has income. Values time. Batch cooks. Tracks macros but doesn't want to build recipes from scratch.
- **Pain:** Three apps open at once. Macros guessed. Nothing prepped at 8pm. Ordered food that wrecked the day's training.
- **Not the target:** Beginners who are overwhelmed by macros. Pure weight-loss dieters. Vegans (small vegan section exists but not the core).

## 4. Pricing Assessment

### Monthly (€16.99 / ~$18.50 USD)

**Position in market:** Sits between MacroFactor ($11.99) and MFP Premium+ ($24.99). This is the right zone.

**Is it underpriced?** No — it's appropriately priced for launch. The monthly plan has 85+ recipes (about half the library), verified macros, and auto grocery list. That functional package sits comfortably at this price point.

**Can it go higher?** Yes. €19.99/mo is achievable once the app is live with reviews and social proof. This is still below MFP Premium+ for a more specialized product. Raise when you hit 100+ monthly-tier recipes.

### Annual (€150 / ~$163 USD)

**Position in market:** This is the highest annual price in the comparable set. PlateJoy and MFP Premium+ are at $99. MacroFactor is at $71.99.

**Is it underpriced?** No — if anything, it requires the strongest value justification because it's asking more than better-known competitors.

**Is the price justified?**
- If the user fully understands what they're getting (full library, exclusive drops, annual-only recipes): **YES**
- If they're comparing on feature count alone vs. MFP Premium+: **needs stronger messaging**

**Key tension:** MFP Premium+ offers 1,500+ recipes + meal planner + grocery list for $99.99/yr. SoulGainz Annual offers 170+ recipes for €150. The winning argument is *specialisation and quality over quantity*. Every SoulGainz recipe is battle-tested, macro-verified, and designed for performance. MFP recipes are user-submitted and unverified. That's the price gap you're selling.

## 5. Brand-Archetype Pricing Fit

Hero brands price at a **premium**. Customers pay for the transformation, not the feature list. The pricing should signal "this is for serious people" — not budget-conscious, not mainstream, not for everyone.

**Conclusion:** €150/yr is directionally correct for a Hero-positioned brand. It signals quality and filters out casual browsers who won't get results anyway.

However: the pricing signal only works if it's backed by social proof (reviews, testimonials, before/after results). Without that, high pricing creates friction instead of trust. At pre-launch stage, this is the risk.

## 7. Messaging Gaps (Pricing Page)

The current pricing page is well-written. Two improvements would directly support the price:

**1. Add proof before the price**
Currently: plan cards → Our Story → FAQ
The "Our Story" section is strong emotional copy, but it comes *after* the price. People read the price, hesitate, then scroll down. Move a single social proof element (even a quote from a beta user, or a macro stat like "average member hits their protein target 6 out of 7 days") *above* the plan cards.

**2. Sharpen the annual card's value proposition**
Current: "The complete 170+ recipe library, exclusive annual-only recipes, and first access to every new drop."
Better: "Every recipe in the library — verified to the gram. Exclusive annual-only drops you won't get anywhere else. One payment, zero stress for a full year."
The current line is accurate but passive. The revision adds specificity and removes commitment anxiety.

**3. Emphasize what you're NOT (differentiation table)**
Consider adding a 2-column comparison: "SoulGainz vs. generic nutrition apps" that highlights verified macros, batch-cook design, high-protein specialization. This justifies the price without naming competitors.

## 9. Summary

SoulGainz has a clear, well-executed brand identity — Hero primary, Sage secondary — that supports premium pricing. The monthly at €16.99 is appropriately positioned for launch. The annual at €150 is the highest in its competitive set and requires strong messaging to justify — the specialisation story (verified macros, high-protein focus, batch-cook design) is the argument, but it needs to be louder on the pricing page.

The product is not underpriced. It is appropriately priced for a pre-launch product with a Hero brand. The path to higher pricing (€19.99/mo, €180/yr) runs through social proof — reviews, results, testimonials — not further copy tweaks.

**Immediate action:** Add "Founding Member" framing to the annual plan. Frame €150 as the introductory rate before launch raises it. This is true and creates urgency without discounting.
