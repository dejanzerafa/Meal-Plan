## Part 1 — DIY Content Creation

### 1.1 Minimum Viable Equipment

You do not need a camera. A modern iPhone or Samsung shoots better food content than most YouTubers used five years ago. Here is the exact setup:

**Phone:**
Any iPhone 13 or later, or Samsung S21 or later. Shoot in **4K 30fps** (not 60fps — motion blur looks more cinematic). Enable ProRes on iPhone if available.

**Stabilisation — pick one:**
- **DJI OM 6 gimbal** (~$120 USD) — eliminates shaky footage on overhead shots and walk-and-talk segments. The single biggest quality upgrade available.
- **Budget option:** A $15 flexible phone tripod (Joby GorillaPod) for static overhead shots.

**Lighting — the make-or-break element for food:**
- **Primary:** A $30–50 LED ring light or a $60 LED panel (Neewer, Godox). Place it at 45° from the food, not directly above.
- **Best free option:** A large north-facing window. Film between 8am–12pm for soft, even light. Qatar mornings work well before heat haze diffuses outdoor light.
- **Avoid:** Fluorescent overhead kitchen lights — they create a yellow cast that makes food look cheap.

**Background/surface:**
- Dark slate/charcoal chopping board (~$20–30) — aligns perfectly with the SoulGainz black palette.
- A piece of dark linen or textured dark grey fabric as a base cloth for flat-lay shots.
- Avoid white countertops or bright tiles — they fight the dark premium aesthetic.

**Microphone (for voiceover/talking content):**
- **Rode Wireless GO II** (~$299) — clips on, works wirelessly, professional audio. Worth it if you plan to do talking-head reels.
- **Budget option:** Record voiceover after filming using your phone's built-in mic in a quiet room. Then layer in post.

**Total minimum spend:** ~$50 (tripod + lighting) → looks good enough.
**Recommended spend:** ~$180 (DJI OM 6 + ring light + dark board) → looks professional.

### 1.2 Filming Tips Specific to Food Content

**Rule 1 — Three angles cover everything:**
1. **Overhead / flat-lay** (0°): for ingredient spreads, container reveals, macro breakdowns.
2. **Side profile** (45°): for plated food with height — bowls, stacked containers.
3. **Close-up** (macro lens or 3x zoom): for texture reveals — the glaze on chicken, steam rising, sauce drizzle.

Every reel should use at least two of these three.

**Rule 2 — Motion beats static.**
Slow, deliberate movement reads as premium. Static shots look like photos. Use:
- Slow tilt from table to food level
- Slow overhead pull-away (start close, drift up)
- Handheld drift across a row of prepped containers
- Pour/drizzle shot in slow motion (240fps, then slow to 30%)

**Rule 3 — Steam and sizzle are your best friends.**
Film within 30 seconds of taking food off heat. Steam = freshness = desire. A sizzling pan shot gets 3–5x more saves than a cold plated shot.

**Rule 4 — Colour contrast on dark backgrounds.**
Orange chicken glaze, red chilli flakes, green herbs, bright yellow yolk — all pop against a dark slate. Style every dish with at least one bright garnish that contrasts the SoulGainz palette.

**Rule 5 — Film containers, not just plated food.**
Your audience is batch cookers. A row of 7 identical glass containers lined up is *peak* SoulGainz content. The organised chaos of a full fridge shelf after a prep session is aspirational to your audience.

**Container recommendation:** Weck glass jars or OXO glass containers. Both photograph beautifully and align with premium positioning. Film the seal click and the label reveal.

### 1.3 Editing Apps

**CapCut (Free — primary recommendation)**
- The current gold standard for Reels editing on mobile.
- Built-in auto-captions (essential — 85% of Reels are watched without sound).
- Speed curves for cinematic slow-motion ramps.
- Text animation presets that can match SoulGainz brand colours (manually set hex codes).
- AI background removal for macro callouts.
- **Template function:** Create one SoulGainz-branded template, then reuse it. Drop in new clips, update numbers, done. This is how you batch 10 reels in one session.
- Trending audio is surfaced in the app — use it to boost initial reach.

**InShot (Free / $4 USD monthly)**
- Better for quick trims and aspect ratio adjustments.
- Good for adding background music with fine-grained volume control.
- Use it alongside CapCut rather than instead of it.

**Adobe Premiere Rush (Free tier available)**
- More precise colour grading — use the Curves tool to push shadows darker and add warmth to midtones. This is how you get that cinematic dark-orange SoulGainz look in post.
- Syncs with desktop Premiere if you ever want to do more complex edits on a laptop.

**Unfold or Canva (Brand overlays)**
- For static text overlays: macro numbers, recipe name, "173 recipes" callouts.
- Build 3–4 reusable text card templates in your brand colours. Drop them in as PNG overlays in CapCut.

**DaVinci Resolve (Desktop — free, advanced)**
- If you have a laptop, this is the most powerful free editing suite available. The colour grading tools (including an orange-teal split that looks stunning on dark food content) are broadcast-quality.
- Steep learning curve — only worth it if you enjoy editing.

### 1.4 Audio Strategy

**Always use trending audio in Reels.** Instagram's algorithm gives a significant distribution boost to videos using popular audio tracks. Check the trending audio tab in Reels weekly.

**SoulGainz audio identity:**
- Dark, driving beats — think gym trap, cinematic lofi, or minimal drill.
- Avoid: upbeat pop, cooking show jingles, anything that reads as "mommy blogger" energy.
- Search CapCut and Instagram's audio library for: "dark cinematic," "gym motivation," "trap beat," "lofi drill."

**Voiceover approach:**
Record voiceovers in a quiet room (bathroom with soft furnishings works well). Keep them under 8 seconds for hook + setup. Speak slowly and confidently — match the brand tone.

### 1.5 Batch Content Creation — Filming 20 Clips in One Session

This is how you stay consistent without letting content creation eat your week. Pick one prep day (Sunday is on-brand) and film everything in one 90-minute session.

**The SoulGainz Batch Session Blueprint:**

**Before filming (30 min):**
- Choose 2–3 recipes you're prepping that week.
- Write out your shot list (see template below).
- Set up your backdrop (dark board + light source).
- Charge phone and gimbal. Clear phone storage.

**Shot list template per recipe (10 clips, ~3 minutes filming each):**
1. Overhead: raw ingredients laid out flat.
2. Close-up: a key ingredient (chicken breast, salmon fillet, eggs).
3. Sizzle shot: protein hitting a hot pan (side angle, 240fps).
4. Overhead: stirring/tossing action.
5. Close-up: sauce pour or glaze application.
6. Steam reveal: lifting a lid (overhead, slow drift back).
7. Plating shot: food being portioned into containers (overhead).
8. Container row: all 7 containers lined up (slow drift across).
9. Fridge load: containers sliding into a full, organised fridge (side angle).
10. Hand grab: reaching in to grab a container (close-up, aspirational).

**With 2–3 recipes filmed:** You have 20–30 raw clips — enough to build 8–12 reels.

**After filming (60 min later that day or the next):**
- Import all clips to CapCut.
- Edit reel 1 fully — grade, text, audio, captions.
- Duplicate the project for reel 2, swap clips, update text. Repeat.
- Export all as drafts in Instagram. Schedule across the next 3 weeks.

**Result:** One 3-hour Sunday session produces 3–4 weeks of Reels content.

## Part 2 — Outsourcing Video Editing

### 2.1 What to Outsource vs. Keep In-House

| Task | DIY or Outsource? | Reason |
|------|-------------------|--------|
| Filming | Always DIY | Only you have access to the food and brand context |
| Basic cuts/trimming | DIY | CapCut makes this <10 min per reel |
| Colour grading | Outsource | If you want a consistent cinematic grade across all content |
| Text animation / motion graphics | Outsource | High-skill, time-consuming |
| Thumbnail/cover frames | Outsource | Canva-level or better |
| Full production reels (scripted) | Outsource | Worth it for 2–4 anchor reels per month |

The smart model: **film everything yourself, outsource the edit on your 4 "hero" reels per month**, DIY the rest.

---

### 2.2 Where to Find Editors

**Fiverr — best for one-off and budget work**
- Search: "food video editor," "short form video editor," "Instagram Reels editor"
- Filter: Top Rated Seller, 4.8+ stars, 50+ reviews
- **Budget tier:** $15–30 per reel (basic cuts, text, music)
- **Mid tier:** $40–80 per reel (colour grade, motion graphics, brand-matched styling)
- **What to look for in their portfolio:** Dark aesthetic edits, food content experience, consistent text animation style. Reject anyone whose portfolio is all bright pastel cooking content.
- **Gig to search:** "cinematic food reel editor" — more specific, better quality matches.

**Upwork — best for ongoing relationships**
- Post a job: "Short-form video editor for Instagram Reels — food/fitness brand"
- Budget: $20–50/reel for a reliable mid-level editor; $50–150/reel for experienced food content specialists.
- Benefit: You build a relationship with one editor who learns the brand over time. Far more efficient at month 3 than month 1.
- **Interview question:** Ask candidates to colour grade one of your raw clips as a paid test ($10–15). Their output tells you more than any portfolio.

**Facebook Groups — underrated, cheaper, faster:**
- Search "Video editors for hire," "Social Media Video Editors," "Short form video editors."
- Many talented editors charge $10–20/reel and are actively building portfolios.
- Risk: higher variance in quality. Always use test edits before committing.

**Direct Instagram DM outreach:**
- Find editors who already make dark-aesthetic food content.
- Search hashtags: `#foodvideographer`, `#reelscreator`, `#foodcontentcreator`
- Many will do collab rates or lower fees in exchange for credit, especially in early stages.

---

### 2.3 What to Send Them (Your Brand Kit)

Create a single Google Drive or Notion folder labelled **SoulGainz — Editor Kit**. Include:

**1. Brand colours (hex codes):**
- Black: `#0C0B0A`
- Orange: `#E07B2A`
- Cream: `#F2EDE6`

**2. Typography:**
- Specify a font (e.g., Bebas Neue for headers, Inter for body). If you don't have a set font, tell them: "Bold sans-serif, clean, gym-brand energy. Not script or serif."

**3. Logo files:**
- SVG + PNG on transparent background. Export from `soulgainz_logo.svg` in the project.

**4. Reference reels (3–5 examples):**
- Find 3–5 Instagram Reels from other accounts whose aesthetic matches the SoulGainz vision. Include links. This communicates more than any written brief.
- Good reference accounts to pull from: gym/dark food aesthetics, premium supplement brands — look for dark-toned food/fitness creators with cinematic editing.

**5. Written brief template per reel:**
```
Reel brief:
Recipe: [name]
Hook text (on screen): "[first 2 seconds text]"
Macro callout: [X kcal · Xg protein · Xg carbs · Xg fat]
CTA text: "173 recipes at soulgainz.app"
Vibe: Dark, premium, cinematic. No bright filters. No jump cuts.
Audio: [link to trending audio OR "editor's choice — dark/gym energy"]
Length: 20–30 seconds
```

**6. Don'ts list:**
- No bright colour correction (no LUT presets that wash out the dark palette)
- No "bubbly" transitions or Star Wipe effects
- No Comic Sans or script fonts
- No text that covers the food for more than 1 second
- No white backgrounds on text cards

### 2.4 Typical Costs and What to Expect

| Service Level | Cost Per Reel | Turnaround | Best For |
|---------------|--------------|------------|---------|
| Fiverr basic | $15–25 | 2–3 days | Quick content, high volume |
| Fiverr mid-tier | $40–70 | 3–5 days | Brand-matched edits |

**Budget model for a solo founder:**
- 4 DIY reels/month (quick CapCut edits): $0

---

### 2.5 Red Flags When Hiring

- No revision policy in their offer — always insist on at least 1 revision included.
- Portfolio has zero food content — domain experience matters for food aesthetics.
- They ask for your Instagram login — never share credentials. Share raw files via Drive only.
- They apply the same LUT preset to everything — you want someone who grades to brief, not to habit.
- No communication within 24 hours of hiring — responsiveness predicts reliability.

## Part 3 — 10 Reel Content Ideas for SoulGainz

These are ordered from highest-effort to lowest, mixed across content pillars.

---

### Reel 1 — "The Sunday Reset" (Anchor reel, monthly)
**Format:** Time-lapse / sped-up prep session with voiceover.
**Hook text:** "One session. Seven days of food. Watch."
**Structure:** Raw fridge (empty) → ingredients out → cooking montage (4–6 cuts) → containers filled → fridge packed → macro label pop.
**CTA:** "173 recipes at soulgainz.app"
**Why it works:** Aspirational prep content is the highest-performing format in the fitness niche. It triggers saves, shares, and follows from people who want to do this but haven't started.
**Length:** 30–45 seconds.

---

### Reel 2 — "Macro Reveal" (Weekly staple)
**Format:** Single recipe, reveal-style. Text builds line by line.
**Hook text:** "This hits [X]g protein. One serving."
**Structure:** Plated dish close-up → macro numbers animate in one by one (kcal, protein, carbs, fat) → ingredient list appears → container row shot → CTA.
**CTA:** "Find it in the app."
**Why it works:** Protein number reveals drive comments ("wait, how?") and saves. Numbers are inherently shareable.
**Length:** 15–20 seconds.

---

### Reel 3 — "Before / After the Prep Session" (Biweekly)
**Format:** Split screen or cut between two states.
**Hook text:** "Before Sunday. After Sunday."
**Structure:** Shot of empty fridge / chaotic kitchen → hard cut to 7 stacked containers, clean surface, organised fridge shelf.
**Voiceover:** "Same kitchen. Ninety minutes. Seven days of food sorted."
**CTA:** "soulgainz.app — cook once, eat all week."
**Why it works:** Transformation format. Before/after is one of the most-saved content structures on the platform.
**Length:** 15–20 seconds.

---

### Reel 4 — "What I Eat in a Day" (Monthly)
**Format:** Day-in-the-life food log, fast cuts.
**Hook text:** "High protein day in Qatar. Everything prepped Sunday."
**Structure:** Morning (pre-workout container from fridge → macro callout) → Midday (lunch container → macro callout) → Afternoon (snack from app) → Evening (dinner container → macro callout) → End card with daily total macros.
**CTA:** "Get the weekly plan at soulgainz.app."
**Why it works:** "What I eat in a day" is consistently one of the most-searched formats in fitness. The Qatar angle adds authenticity and geographic differentiation — you're the MENA rep for clean eating.
**Length:** 30–40 seconds.

---

### Reel 5 — "The Protein Math" (Education reel)
**Format:** Text-forward, CapCut animated numbers.
**Hook text:** "Most people are 80g short. Every. Single. Day."
**Structure:** Stat appears → "The average person eats ~70g protein" → "If you're training, you need 150–180g" → "That's 80g you're not hitting" → "That's why results stall" → SoulGainz solution.
**CTA:** "86% of our 173 recipes hit 30g+ protein per serving."
**Why it works:** Education content with a clear villain (the gap) and a clear solution. Gets shares from people tagging training partners.
**Length:** 20–25 seconds.

---

### Reel 6 — "5 Containers, 5 Days" (Tutorial-adjacent)
**Format:** Recipe walkthrough, fast and efficient.
**Hook text:** "One recipe. Five days of lunch. Done."
**Structure:** Ingredients laid flat (2 sec) → cooking highlight (4–5 cuts, 8 sec total) → portioning into 5 containers (3 sec) → macro callout per container → containers stacked in fridge (2 sec).
**CTA:** "Full method in the app. 173 recipes."
**Why it works:** Practical, actionable. People who are on the fence about batch cooking see how achievable it is.
**Length:** 20–30 seconds.

---

### Reel 7 — "The Fridge Check" (Weekly engagement reel)
**Format:** Aesthetic fridge reveal, slow pan.
**Hook text:** "Sunday fridge. Don't @ me."
**Structure:** Open fridge door (slow, satisfying) → slow pan across stacked, labelled containers → close-up on one container label → close door → text: "Week sorted."
**No voiceover needed.** Let the visual and ambient sound do the work.
**Why it works:** Extremely high save rate. Organisation content and "aesthetic fridge" content have massive audiences. Highly replicable — you can do this every week with 20 seconds of filming.
**Length:** 10–15 seconds.

---

### Reel 8 — "The Ingredient Swap" (Nutrition education)
**Format:** Side-by-side comparison.
**Hook text:** "Stop eating this. Eat this instead."
**Structure:** Left: standard ingredient (e.g. full-fat yoghurt, white rice, 20% mince) → Right: SoulGainz swap (0% Greek yoghurt, wholegrain brown rice, 5% lean mince) → protein/calorie delta shown → "Same meal. Better macros."
**CTA:** "All swaps built into SoulGainz recipes."
**Why it works:** Shareable, saves-driven. Nutrition hacks get shared by people who want to help their followers.
**Length:** 20–25 seconds.

---

### Reel 9 — "The Founder's Sunday" (BTS / Trust reel)
**Format:** Vlog-style, casual but edited.
**Hook text:** "Solo founder. Built an app. Still preps his own food."
**Structure:** Short clips of the actual prep session with a brief voiceover about the app — why it was built, the problem it solved, one honest moment about the solo founder grind.
**Voiceover example:** "Built SoulGainz because I was spending three hours on Sundays just figuring out what to cook. Now it's 90 minutes, seven days sorted. 173 recipes later — this is what Sunday looks like."
**Why it works:** Authenticity is underused in this niche. The founder story builds parasocial trust that no ad can replicate.
**Length:** 30–45 seconds.

---

### Reel 10 — "Name That Macro" (Engagement reel)
**Format:** Gamified quiz format.
**Hook text:** "Guess the macros. Most people are way off."
**Structure:** Show a dish (no text) → "How much protein do you think is in this?" → Hold 3 seconds → Reveal: macro breakdown animates in.
**Run this as a series:** Same format, different recipe each week. The audience starts guessing in comments.
**Why it works:** Comments spike = algorithm boost. Simple to produce — one dish shot, CapCut text animation. Zero cooking footage required if the dish is already prepped.
**Length:** 15–20 seconds.

## Quick-Reference: CapCut Workflow for One Reel

1. Import clips → trim to best moments
2. Set speed: slow-motion sections to 40–50%, action cuts to 100–120%
3. Colour grade: Exposure -10, Contrast +15, Warmth +8, Shadow -20 (dark, warm)
4. Add trending audio at 30–40% volume
5. Add voiceover (if applicable) at 80% volume
6. Auto-caption → style captions in Cream `#F2EDE6` on black background
7. Text overlays: recipe name, macros, CTA — all in brand colours
8. Export: 1080x1920, MP4, maximum quality
9. Post to Reels, add cover frame (first clean shot of plated food)
