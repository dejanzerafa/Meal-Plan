## Two findings that apply to the whole batch

### 1. None of the posted macros can be imported

All 36 pass an internal Atwater check (4P + 4C + 9F ≈ stated kcal). That is not
reassurance — it means the four numbers were generated as a set. It says nothing
about whether they describe the food. Computed bottom-up from USDA reference
values, six of six spot-checks failed the 7% audit threshold:

| Recipe | Claimed | Computed | Worst error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crispy Gochujang Chicken | 480 / 42 / 52 / 9 | 594 / 52 / 66 / 12 | **+34%** fat |
| Greek Chicken Skewers | 455 / 45 / 15 / 24 | 361 / 51 / 8 / 13 | **−46%** fat |
| Bake Once, Eat 6 Times | 420 / 45 / 12 / 20 | 358 / 44 / 6 / 17 | **−53%** carbs |
| Big Mac Bowl | 525 / 40 / 43 / 21 | 533 / 52 / 40 / 19 | +31% protein |
| Loaded Burger Bowl | 690 / 48 / 52 / 30 | 740 / 56 / 55 / 33 | +16% protein |
| Creamy Chicken & Gnocchi | 480 / 42 / 52 / 9 | 508 / 48 / 57 / 8 | +14% protein |

The proof it is systematic rather than sloppy: **Crispy Gochujang Chicken and
Creamy Chicken & Gnocchi carry byte-identical macros** — 480/42/52/9/2 — while
sharing no main ingredient. One is a template artifact. The gnocchi figures are
roughly right by luck; the gochujang figures are 24% low.

Consequence: every surviving recipe gets its macros **computed from its
ingredients**, with the posted numbers used only as a cross-check. Importing as
published would break "verified to the gram" on the first recipe a user logged.

### 2. Provenance — rebuild, do not transcribe

Two of the four sources sell against you: `workit.app` is a competitor app, and
`healthyeatingfeed` sells a cookbook off these posts. Ingredient lists and
functional method steps are not copyrightable; the written prose is — every
"The verdict", "Why You'll Repeat It" and tip paragraph belongs to them.

So: dish concepts only. Quantities rebuilt from scratch against SoulGainz macro
targets, all copy written fresh. The macro failures above force a rebuild
anyway, so this costs nothing extra.
