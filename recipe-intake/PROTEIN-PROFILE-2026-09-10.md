# The staged batch dilutes the protein profile — a release decision

Written while checking three portion sizes (m121, m162, m188). Those three
turned out not to be the story.

**Protein as a share of calories** is the honest way to compare recipes of
different sizes. Here is the live library against the 230 staged recipes:

| Category | Live median | Staged median | Live floor | Staged below that floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| main | 37% | 32% | 21% | **19** of 105 |
| breakfast | 35% | 22% | 30% | **29** of 40 |
| salad | 42% | 25% | 35% | **20** of 32 |
| dessert | 33% | 23% | 23% | **9** of 17 |
| smoothie | 38% | 46% | 29% | **3** of 36 |

Smoothies are the exception, and they explain the rest: during intake, 30 g of
whey was added to every smoothie. Nothing equivalent was done for the other
categories, and those are the ones that came out short.

## The three that started this

- `m121` Chicken, Sweet Potato & Cannellini Soup — **not a defect.** 1015 g looks
  huge, but 350 g of it is broth and the soup is blended. 638 kcal at 51.3 g
  protein is 32% — right at the live median. Leave it.
- `m162` Curried Chickpea Bowl (16%) and `m188` Chickpea Coconut Curry (15%) are
  not outliers in kind, only in size — they are two of nineteen staged mains
  below the floor. Cutting their chickpeas makes them **worse**: chickpeas are
  the only protein in both, so 370 g → 164 g takes m162 from 16% to 13%.

## What I would do

Release is already per-recipe, so this costs nothing to act on: hold the ones
below the floor and release the rest. The alternative — a second intake pass
that lifts them the way the smoothies were lifted — is real work and changes
recipes you have not reviewed.
